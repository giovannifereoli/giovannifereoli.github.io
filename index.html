Giovanni Fereoli

Ph.D. Student in Aerospace Engineering Sciences · University of Colorado Boulder

Giovanni Fereoli is a Ph.D. student in the Ann and H.J. Smead Department of Aerospace Engineering Sciences at the University of Colorado Boulder, where he works in the Orbital Research Cluster for Celestial Applications (ORCCA) Laboratory under the supervision of Dr. Jay W. McMahon. He is also a Flight Dynamics Affiliate at the Laboratory for Atmospheric and Space Physics (LASP), where he supports navigation for the Emirates Mission to the Asteroid Belt and contributes to Scarabaeus, an open-source interplanetary orbit determination software.

His research develops orbit determination methods for small-body missions, addressing its three components: the dynamical model, the measurement model, and the estimation framework. This includes interior gravity field modeling with cylindrical harmonics, map-free terrain-relative optical navigation based on visual odometry, and Bayesian inference via Markov Chain Monte Carlo accelerated by high-order state transition tensors. The methods are validated on data from NASA's Dawn mission and on scenarios inspired by the NEAR Shoemaker and OSIRIS-REx missions, and will be applied to ESA's Hera mission during a 2027 research visit to the University of Bologna.

Giovanni received his M.S. in Space Engineering (110/110 cum Laude) and his B.S. in Aerospace Engineering from Politecnico di Milano. His M.S. thesis on meta-reinforcement learning for spacecraft proximity operations, carried out at the AVS Laboratory at CU Boulder, was published in the Journal of Spacecraft and Rockets. He received the John V. Breakwell Student Award at the 2025 AAS/AIAA Astrodynamics Specialist Conference.

Research Areas

Dynamical Model

Cylindrical Harmonic Gravity Fields

A Fourier–Bessel interior gravity representation that remains valid down to the surface, enabling near-surface gravity science and interior density inference.
Measurement Model

Map-Free Visual Odometry

Epipolar constraints processed directly within orbit determination, without landmark catalogs, and with estimation of the body's rotational state. Validated on Dawn imagery of Vesta.
Estimation Framework

Bayesian Orbit Determination

Ensemble Markov Chain Monte Carlo accelerated by high-order state transition tensors, providing full non-Gaussian posteriors for small-body orbit determination.
